## Study Information

**Title**
Does Bagging Bananas in Paper Accelerate Peel Browning? A Matched-Pair Randomized Experiment

**Authors**
Jingtian Hu

**Description**
A practicum experiment for GU4724 Experimental Research (Columbia University, Fall 2026). Thirty-two bananas from six hands (three conventional, three USDA Organic) are paired within hand on baseline percent brown measured from Day-0 photos. In each pair, one banana is randomly assigned to be stored alone in a closed kraft paper bag at room temperature, and the other is left uncovered in the same room. The primary outcome is the percent of peel area that is brown, measured from standardized photos on Day 3 (72 h after bagging). Inference uses randomization inference over all 2^16 possible assignments.

**Hypotheses**
H1: Storing a banana in a closed paper bag increases the percent of peel area that is brown on the primary measurement day, relative to leaving it uncovered. The hypothesis is directional, but the test is two-sided at α = 0.05. Proposed mechanism: a paper bag traps the ethylene the fruit emits and so speeds ripening. The treatment also changes humidity and light, so the experiment identifies the effect of the bagging bundle, not of ethylene alone.

## Design Plan

**Study type**
Experiment — a researcher randomly assigns units to treatment conditions.

**Blinding**
Check: "No blinding is involved in this study." (The other options concern human subjects or analysts not knowing the assignment, and neither applies. The partial measurement blinding goes in the next field.)

**Is there any additional blinding in this study?**
The researcher places the bags and therefore knows each unit's assignment. Measurement is blinded as far as possible. Photo file names carry only the banana ID, never the arm. The primary outcome is computed by a fixed image-analysis script (`R/03_measure_photos.R`, parameters frozen at the registered commit) that never sees the assignment. The secondary 1–7 ripeness grade is assigned by a rater who does not know the assignment.

**Study design**
Matched-pair (blocked) randomized design with 16 pairs and two arms: paper bag vs. uncovered. All 32 bananas are kept in one room in 16 slots, each made of two letter-size white sheets side by side, one banana per sheet (slots randomly assigned to pairs; layout: `[FILL]`), with at least 10 cm between any two objects (actual: `[FILL]`), away from other fruit, in a ventilated room. Both members of a pair share a slot (left/right). Between Day 0 and Day 3 no banana is touched; treated bananas stay sealed in their bags. On Day 3 every banana is taken out, photographed on both sides, and the experiment ends.

**Randomization**
Implemented in R with randomizr.
(1) No pilot banana: all 32 bananas are in the experiment.
(2) The image-analysis parameters of `R/03_measure_photos.R` were calibrated on the Day-0 photos of all 32 bananas, before assignment and before any outcome existed, against the researcher's visual ratings (Spearman 0.78 over 64 photos). They are frozen at the registered commit and will not be changed. Day-0 percent brown (brown0_v1) is computed for all bananas with this script.
(3) Within each of the 6 hands (6/5/7/5/3/6 bananas), sort by brown0_v1, then by mean hue of non-brown peel, then weight if recorded, and pair adjacent bananas. In each of the four hands with an odd count one banana is set aside at random; the four set-aside bananas are paired across hands within organic status (conventional with conventional, organic with organic), sorted by brown0_v1. The result is 16 pairs.
(4) `block_ra(blocks = pair)` (seed 20261104) assigns exactly one banana per pair to treatment, with probability 1/2.
(5) Table slots are randomly assigned to pairs, and left/right within a slot is randomized.
Disclosure: `02_randomize.R` was test-run on these baseline data with the same seed before registration while debugging the pipeline, so the registered run reproduces that test run exactly. The assignment is generated by the script with no manual input.

## Sampling Plan

**Existing data**
Registration prior to creation of data. Baseline (pre-treatment) covariates have been recorded, but no outcome data exist.

**Explanation of existing data**
Baseline photos and hand membership were recorded on Day 0 before assignment, and the measurement parameters were calibrated on them. They are used for pairing and covariate adjustment. No outcome data exist.

**Data collection procedures**
The sample is the 32 bananas bought on 2026-11-03 at one Trader Joe's store in New York, NY: all bananas from 6 hands, 18 conventional (hands of 6, 5 and 7) and 14 USDA Organic (hands of 5, 3 and 6). No banana was excluded. The bananas were refrigerated for about `[FILL]` hours after purchase and then kept at room temperature for about `[FILL]` hours before the Day-0 photos. Both arms share this history because it happened before assignment. On Day 0 (2026-11-03) the hands were separated at the crown, each banana was labeled 1–32 on the stem, and both sides were photographed. Treatment starts after randomization on the evening of Day 0. On Day 3 (2026-11-06, about 17:00) both sides of every banana are photographed again under the same desk lamp, on the same white paper background, with the phone at a fixed height and auto-HDR off; the Day-3 photos are also rated by a blind rater. Bag condition is checked visually each day without opening the bags, and room temperature is logged daily. Bags are standard kraft paper lunch bags, one per treated banana. Data collection lasts 3 days.

**Sample size**
32 bananas, 16 pairs.

**Sample size rationale**
The sample size is fixed by the number of bananas bought. In a simulation (SD = 15 pp, constant effect, α = .05 two-sided, randomization-inference test, 2,000 simulated datasets), 16 pairs give power of 0.69 (within-pair correlation ρ = 0.5) to 0.90 (ρ = 0.7) for a 10 percentage-point effect, and 0.42 if pairing is uninformative (ρ = 0). The minimum detectable effect at 80% power with ρ = 0.6 is about 10 pp. Code: `R/01_power.R`.

**Stopping rule**
Data collection ends after the Day 3 photos. No interim analysis of outcomes is done.

## Variables

**Manipulated variables**
Z = 1: the banana is stored alone in one closed kraft paper bag, opening folded twice. Z = 0: the banana is uncovered in its table slot. Assignment is at the individual-banana level within pairs.

**Measured variables**
- **Primary outcome:** percent of visible peel area that is brown on Day 3 (72 h after bagging), averaged over the two photographed sides. Computed by `R/03_measure_photos.R` at the registered commit: peel pixels are identified by saturation and hue; a peel pixel is brown if its brightness is below 0.75 times the brightest peel within about 1 cm and its hue is below 0.115, or its hue is below 0.09, or its brightness is below 0.3. The parameters are frozen and will not be re-tuned.
- **Secondary outcomes:** (i) blind-rated 1–7 ripeness grade on the Day-3 photos; (ii) `brown_index`, a threshold-free mean darkness of peel relative to local clean peel, from the same script.
- **Baseline covariates:** hand (1–6), organic (0/1), and Day-0 percent brown (brown0_v1, used both for pairing and as the regression covariate). - **Process data:** daily bag condition (intact / torn / open), room temperature.

**Indices**
The primary outcome is the mean of the two side-specific percent-brown values for each banana on Day 3.

## Analysis Plan

**Statistical models**
Primary estimator: the mean of the 16 within-pair differences (treated − control) in the primary outcome. With equal pair sizes and p = 1/2, this equals the block-weighted difference in means (Gerber & Green 2012, eq. 3.10). Standard error: √[Σ(dⱼ − d̄)² / (16 × 15)].
Secondary, covariate-adjusted: `estimatr::lm_robust(Y ~ Z + brown0_c, fixed_effects = ~ pair)`, where brown0_c is centered baseline percent brown. Reported next to the primary estimate, which remains the main result.
Secondary outcomes are analyzed with the same estimator and test.

**Transformations**
None. Baseline percent brown is mean-centered for the covariate-adjusted model only.

**Inference criteria**
Sharp null hypothesis of no effect for any banana. Randomization inference enumerates all 2^16 = 65,536 within-pair assignments with `ri2::conduct_ri` and the full permutation matrix. Two-sided p-value = share of assignments with |estimate| ≥ |observed|. Reject at p ≤ 0.05. 95% CI: assume a constant effect equal to the estimate, impute the potential outcomes, and take the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the randomization distribution. There is no multiple-comparison correction, and secondary outcomes are labeled secondary and not used for the main conclusion.

**Data exclusion**
No exclusions. A banana that rots or splits stays in the data and is scored 100% brown from that day on.

**Missing data**
Analysis is intention-to-treat by original assignment. A torn or opened bag is logged but does not change the unit's arm. If a banana is lost (eaten, dropped, destroyed), its pair is dropped from the primary analysis, and extreme-value bounds are reported with the missing value set to 0% and to 100% brown.

**Exploratory analysis (optional)**
(a) Treatment effects by hand, by organic vs. conventional hands, and by baseline ripeness above vs. below the median. Descriptive only.
(b) For each control banana, the number of uncovered neighbors within 30 cm, as a descriptive check on ethylene spillover between units.
